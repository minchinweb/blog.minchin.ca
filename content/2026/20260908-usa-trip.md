Last month we completed a trip to the States (Idaho, specifically). One of
things trips do is throw the day-to-day routine up for examination, as you
build one for the trip. So it makes me notice things that are otherwise just
"normal". Here's some of the things I noticed this trip:

- **US Border wait times got extreme**: normally we cross at Coutts/Sweatgrass,
  and in planning our trip, it's always a bit of question mark of how much time
  to expect to spend here, but it's often less than 10 minutes, and half hour
  on the outside. Previously, the longest I remeber being stuck at the border
  was one time I was driving northbound in late March and got stuck in a line
  of Snowbirds returning north in the motorhomes.

    This time, *we were stuck for 2 hours!* If there would have been a way to
    get out of line, I was seriously considering 80 km (and back) to Del Bonita
    to cross there.

    When we did finally got to the front of the line, I asked if the delay was
    expectional and was told "no", that it had been this way for the last three
    months, as they merge the trucks and cars into a single lane every afternoon
    at 3:45 pm (we were crossing ~9 pm).

    I guess for the future we need to cross in by the mid-afternoon or plan on
    driving through Carway (south of Cardston) or Del Bonita instead (but
    confirm their hours, as I don't think either are open 24/7 like Coutts).

- Montana has a ton of licence plate designs, which makes it hard to tell at a
  glance which plates are "Montana plates".
- We play the "license plate game" driving, which involves trying to find a
  license plate from each province and state as we travel. I was surprised at
  the number of Maine plates we say (always on the back of a semi-truck
  trailer), given it's distance from where we were and realitively small
  population. On the flip side, I was surprised to *not* see Quebec, given it's
  population.
- **Rural Idaho has way more houses that local argiculture should support**.
  Maybe it's become the playground of hobby farmers, or maybe people are opting
  to live in the countryside and then just commuting to jobs in large centers
  (I actually have a Aunt that lives like this and then drives the hour to work
  in the hour to the far northern suburbs of Salt Lake City for work).
- **Hotels seems way more expensive than they have the right to be.** The
  *Great American Roadtrip* is a vacation, but part of the appeal was
  (historically?) that it was relatively affordable. I recorded prices during
  my [2009 trip to
  Quebec]({filename}../2009/20090424-to-quebec-and-adventure.md) (much of which
  was driving across the States), and so can compare costs:

    - **meals** (per person): average of $9.91 in 2009[^2026090801] vs $18.82
      in 2026[^2026090802], so an increase of ~2x.
    - **gas**: average of $26.25 in 2009[^2026090803] vs $72.27
      today[^2026090804], so an increase of ~3x. As it happens, I am driving
      the same generation of the same model of minivan on both trips, so tank
      size and fuel effiecincy should be very comparable.
    - **hotels**: $38 to $47 in 2009[^2026090805] vs $140 to $150 in
      2006[^2026090806], so an increase of ~3x. Furthermore, the hotels in 2009
      typically included breakfast for the price while neither of the 2026
      hotels did. In 2026, to find a hotel including breakfast roughly doubled
      the price.
  
    This also hides the exchange rate, which adds another ~10%[^2026090807] to
    these price bumps. So the trip comes in ~3x as expense as it was in 2009,
    in Canadian dollars. Compare to the official inflation numbers, which are
    1/6 as much[^2026090808].

    The alternative is flying five of us down and renting a car at our
    destination, so I suspect we'll keep driving for now, but the price bump is
    crazy to see laid out.

- also on inflation, I don't think the Canadian personal exemption (i.e. what
  you're allowed to bring back across the border without paying import duties)
  has changed in the same time. It was CAD $800 in 2010, and is still there
  today....
- Banff is absolutely gorgeious. It's been a while since I've been, and it's
  easy to start assuming that the hype is marketing, but the mountain views
  really do live up to the hype. I just wish it wasn't quite as busy so we
  could see more of it.
