title: 20 Yars of Blogging
date: 2026-04-19

In looking at my blog archives, I realized I
started this blog 20 years ago this month!

At the time, blogging [already felt
well-worn]({filename}/2006/20060425-the-history-of-blogs.md),
but blogs were still mostly personal affairs. It
was the era before Twitter and corporate blogs
(both as magazine replacements and company PR).
Both those have faded away, or ay least from the
spotlight, and many of the current blogs I run onto
now are minalist technical/personal hybrids.

Since that time, Google annoucements have good from
being received with excitement (e.g. GMail) to
apprehesion. Soon after launching, I placed Google
Ads on the site. They were small, text only, amd
relatively unobtrusive. They never paid super well,
but I used to rack up some earnings each year.
However, I never made it to thr minimum $100 in
earnings required for payout, and the balance
stopped going up, so they've been off for a couple
years at this point (maybe since the [Seafoam
redesign]({filename}/2016/20160912-i-redesigned-my-blog.md), in 2016?).

I don't know if I've ever really found a common
theme or a groove for posts. About half of my posts
were my [Project 365]({tag}Project 365) (that I
only made to photo 100), ca. 2010. I've documented
a [trip across the continent]({tag}Quebec Trip
2009) (Alberta to Quebec, summer of 2009). I've
[bemoaned the changes in
Facebook]({filename}/2014/20140204-the-transformation-of-facebook.md)
(back in 2014...). I'm still looking for a better
way to [manage my to-do
list]({filename}/20080323-to-do-could-do-should-do.md).
Of late, I've started [documenting a new (old)
business]({tag}Strathcona Power). The time it takes
to write, even this post, even when I have ideas I
want to share, keeps the number of posts written
low. A big part of that is I want the posts to be
(at least mostly) well thought out and cohertant.

Technologically, the blog has only ever had two
backends: Blogger and [Github
Pages]({filename}/2012/20120323-free-hosting-for-static-websites.md)
/+ Pelican. The orginal draw of GitHub Pages was
free hosting, and that has held out (although
Blogger would still be free too). Pelican, as a
site generator, continues to work well and
continues to be maintained, with no unsolved issues
(for my workflows).

Design-wise, I was pushing the limits of the
Blogger template system, which is part of why I
moved on. The initial Pelican design was a port of
the (then current) Blogger design, but it didn't
take long for me to roll out a new "modern" design
(*[seafoam]({tag}Seafoam)*), based on Bootstrap. A
major goal of that redesign was to better support
my blog on (narrow) phones, which just weren't a
thing when I started! 17+ interations later, that's
still the design in use.

So what does the next 20 years hold? I guess you'll
have to set a bookmark and check back!

P.S. [My
thoughts]({filename}/2007/20070620-seven-things-ive-learned-blogging.md)
from one year into this adventure, still ring
mostly true.
