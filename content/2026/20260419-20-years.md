title: 20 Yars of Blogging
date: 2026-04-19

In looking at my blog archives, I realized I started this blog 20 years ago this month!

At the time, blogging already felt well-worn, but blogs were still mostly personal affairs. It was the era before Twitter and corporate blogs (both as magazine replacements and company PR). Both those have faded away, and many of the current blogs are minalist technical/personal hybrids.

Since that time, Google annoucements have good from being received with excitement (e.g. GMail) to apprehesion. Soon after launching, I placed Google Ads on the site. They were small, text only, amd relatively unobtrusive. They never paid super well, but I used to rack up some earnings each year. However, I never madenit to thr minimum $100 in earnings required for payout, and thr balance stopped going up, so they've been off for a couple years at this point (maybe simce the Seafoam redesign?).

I don't know if I've ever really found a theme or a groove for posts. About half of my posts were my Project 365 (that I only made to photo 100). I've documented a trip across the continent. I've bemoaned the changes in Facebook (back in 2014...). Of late, I've started documenting a new (old) business. The time it takes to write, even this post, even when I have ideas I want to share, keeps the number ofbposts written down. A big part of that is I want tge posts to be (at least mostly) well thought out and cohertant.

Technologically, the blog has only ever had two backends: Blogger and Github Pages + Pelican. The orginal draw of GitHub Pages was free hosting, and that has held out (although Blogger would still be free too). Pelican, as a site generator, continues to work well, with no unsolved issues.

Design-wise, I was pushing the limits of the Blogger template system, which is part of why I moved. The initial Pelican design was a port of the (then current) Blogger design, but it didn't take long for me to roll out a new "modern" design, based on Bootstrap. A major goal of that redesign was to better support my blog on phones, which just weren't a thing when I started! 17+ intwrations, that's still the design in use.

So what does the next 20 years hold? I guess you'll have to set a bookmark and check back!
